Methodology

  1. Imagery. Every Sentinel-2 L2A pass over tile 43REP in the season, read from Element84's public cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs at 20 m. Clouds masked with the scene classification layer.
  2. Automatic labels. A pixel is a confident burn when it turns charcoal-dark in visible and short-wave infrared light, keeps the flat SWIR signature of char, and changed since its last clear look. Bright straw, soil and green crop are confident non-burns. Everything else is left unlabelled.
  3. U-Net. A 7.8 M-parameter U-Net reads the before and after images (11 channels) and labels the uncertain fields from shape and context. 20% of 5 km blocks are held out. Every detection must also pass a loose char test.
  4. Matching. Each burn scar (≥ 0.4 ha) is checked against VIIRS S-NPP, NOAA-20 and MODIS Terra/Aqua detections inside each detection's own pixel footprint plus 150 m, within the burn window. A scar with no match is undetected.
  5. Geostationary check. ISRO's INSAT-3DR active-fire product (MOSDAC, 3RIMG_L2P_FIR) flags fire pixels from the 3.9 µm and 10.8 µm brightness temperatures of every 30-minute scan, day and night, so it covers the hours the polar satellites miss. We count its fire pixels over Punjab and the study districts in every season.
  6. Official records. TinyFish reads the CREAMS daily bulletins (counts, district tables, GPS of every counted event), the CPCB AQI bulletins and enforcement news.

Limitations

  • An undetected scar is not proof of evening burning: a fire can also be small, short-lived or hidden by smoke at overpass time.
  • Burnt area is a lower bound. A field burnt and tilled between two clear passes leaves no char to detect.
  • INSAT-3DR pixels are about 4 km (≈1,600 ha), and its fire product was built for forest fires: a pixel is flagged only when enough of it burns hot. A fire burning for an hour appears in two scans.
  • District borders are the pre-2021 geoBoundaries version; "Sangrur" includes Malerkotla. Official counts are summed the same way.
  • PM2.5 is an order-of-magnitude estimate: 6.5 t straw/ha (PPCB), 80% combusted (IPCC), 8.3 g PM2.5/kg (Kim Oanh et al., 2011).
